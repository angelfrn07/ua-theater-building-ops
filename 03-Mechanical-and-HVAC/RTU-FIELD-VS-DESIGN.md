# UA Theater — what is ACTUALLY on the roof vs what the 1998 drawings specified

Written 2026-08-18. This file exists because `RTU-ZONE-MAP.md` lists the **1998 design
intent** (all Lennox), and that is no longer what is up there. Roughly half the roof was
re-equipped with **Trane** units around 2015-2016. If someone orders a part or a
replacement off the zone map alone, they will order the wrong brand.

**Sources, both VERIFIED by reading the documents directly:**

1. **Design:** 1998 EnviroDesign RTU SCHEDULE, sheet **M4.1** (schedule dated 3/10/98),
   in `1998-UA-Amarillo-Original-MEP-EnviroDesign-8-sheets.pdf`. 26 rows. The sheet's own
   heating total (5,726 / 4,582 MBH) sums exactly across those 26 rows, which confirms
   the table is complete and nothing was cropped.
2. **Field:** `AMA Star HVAC Report-November 2023.pdf` — Ron Word, HVAC/R Manager,
   Allen's Tri-State Mechanical, Amarillo, (806) 376-8345, rword@allentri.com. Sent to
   Kathi Gillman at Cinergy, forwarded to Angel 2026-08-17. Ron walked the roof and wrote
   down make, age, model, serial and whether each unit fired.

## The headline

- **26 units designed** in 1998, all Lennox, all 460V 3-phase, 243 nominal tons total.
- **25 units inspected** in Nov 2023. Of those, **13 were still the original Lennox**
  (reported as 24-25 years old) and **12 had been replaced with Trane** (11 reported as
  8 years old, 1 as 5 years old).
- Every Trane went in at the **same nominal tonnage as the Lennox it replaced** — the
  Trane model numbers decode to exactly the tons the 1998 schedule called for in that
  slot. Nobody resized the system.
- **5 units had named faults in 2023.** See the fault list below.

The 8-year-old Trane units line up with the **2016 recliner conversion** that Sam Patton
listed in the building's history. That was almost certainly when they were swapped.

## The three untagged units, and the one nobody looked at

Ron logged three units with **no unit number** on them. The 1998 schedule has exactly four
tags that never appear in his report: **1A, 2, 6A, 8A**. So three of those four are the
untagged units and one was never inspected.

- Untagged **Trane TSC120H4RMA00D0A1, s/n 18261312BL, 5 yr** — TSC120 is 10 tons.
  Of the four missing tags only **RTU-2** is a 10-ton slot (1A and 6A are 4 tons,
  8A is 7.5). **INFERENCE, high confidence: this is RTU-2, Aud 102**, and it is the
  newest unit on the building (~2018-2021).
- Untagged **Lennox, 24 yr, s/n 5699D02422 — bad blower motor**
- Untagged **Lennox, 24 yr, s/n 5699D02421 — fired, no issues**
  Both are the same vintage and serial run as RTU-9A (5699D02423) and RTU-14A
  (5699D02420), which are the 4-ton GCS20 units. **INFERENCE: these two are RTU-1A and
  RTU-6A**, the other two 4-ton units. Which is which is not determinable from paper.
- That leaves **RTU-8A (7.5 ton, Aud 108 / IMAX) as the one unit with no field record
  at all.** Nobody has looked at it since at least 2023. Put it on the walk list.

Sheet note 11 on ME1.1 says every unit was supposed to be stenciled in 2" black letters
with its number and the area served. On the units above, that stencil is gone or was
never restored after the swap. **Re-stenciling all 26 during the first roof walk is
cheap and permanently fixes this ambiguity.**

## The five faults from 2023, worst first

These are 2023 conditions. `RTU-CONDITION.md` carries the 2026 Texas Air picture, which
is worse in places. Nothing here is confirmed still-broken today.

1. **RTU-17A, projection booth**, 12.5 ton Lennox — outside air dampers damaged, and Ron
   **left the unit switched off.** If the booth has been hot ever since, this is why.
2. **RTU-16, entry vestibule**, 12.5 ton Lennox — needs a transformer and fuses. By the
   2026 Texas Air visit this unit had **no power at all** (breaker DHC-55,57,59).
3. **RTU-9A, Aud 109**, 4 ton Lennox — bad blower motor. Aud 109's other unit (9B) was
   fine in 2023, so that room was running on half capacity.
4. **RTU-5, Aud 105**, 8.5 ton Lennox — bad combustion fan motor. That is a heating-side
   failure; it would still cool.
5. **Untagged Lennox s/n 5699D02422** (likely RTU-1A or 6A) — bad blower motor.

Every one of the five is an **original 1998 Lennox**. Not one Trane unit had a fault in
2023. The failure line in this building runs straight along equipment age.

## What this means for money

- The 13 surviving Lennox units are **27-28 years old as of 2026**. That is past the end
  of a commercial RTU's normal service life, and parts for the GCS20/GCS24/LGA series are
  increasingly obsolete.
- The 12 Trane units are **~11 years old** and have a decade of life left.
- So the realistic capital question is not "replace the HVAC," it is **"replace the 13
  remaining Lennox units, on a schedule."** That is the number to put in front of a
  lender or a grant application, and the tonnage for each is in the table above.
- The split, using design tonnage, checks against the schedule total:

  | Group | Units | Nominal tons |
  |---|---|---|
  | Original Lennox still in place (2023) | 13 | 93.5 |
  | RTU-8A, never inspected, presumed still Lennox | 1 | 7.5 |
  | Trane replacements (~2016) | 12 | 142.0 |
  | **Whole roof** | **26** | **243.0** |

  So the aging-Lennox exposure is **93.5 tons across 13 units**, or **101 tons across 14**
  if RTU-8A turns out to still be original. That is the number for a lender, a grant
  application, or a replacement bid — not the 243.

## Not to be confused with the heat pump sheet

Sheet **ME1.2** in the 1998 set is titled "M&E Roof Plan - **Bid Alternate (Heat Pump
Option)**" and carries its own RTU schedule. **That alternate was not built.** The units
on the roof are gas/electric, which both the base schedule (gas input/output MBH per unit)
and Ron Word's 2023 report (gas-fired, combustion fan, inducer) confirm. Ignore ME1.2
except as history.
